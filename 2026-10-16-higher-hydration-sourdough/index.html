Higher-Hydration Sourdough

Oct 16–17, 2026 · wetter than the baseline · flat but beautifully blistered

Ratio

  • Same base as the Oct 12–13 bake (500 g bread flour, 335 g kefir, 8 g water, 100 g rye-flour starter at peak) — but mixed noticeably wetter this time; new ratio not recorded.

Method notes

  • 1-hour fermentolyse in the microwave (off), approx. 7:11–8:12 PM MDT.
  • Salt mixed in after the fermentolyse; dough was clearly higher hydration — slack and glossy.
  • Stretch-and-folds: one round ran late (“got distracted”), then back on track. The folds pulled the dough together — much smoother and holding its shape after.
  • After the final folds, dough went outside for the night — Alberta October cold retard (free refrigeration).
  • Oct 17: back inside and risen, shaped at 7:07 AM; first loaf into a preheated Dutch oven at 8:26 AM; lid off at 8:46 AM, out at 9:06 AM (20 min covered, 20 min uncovered).
  • Second loaf into the Dutch oven ~4:18 PM; lid off at 4:38 PM (20 min covered); out of the oven by 5:01 PM.

Photos

Result: A little flatter than the last bake — the overnight outdoor cold retard likely over-fermented the wetter dough. But the long retard paid off in the crust: deep golden color and gorgeous blistering. The crumb came out even, super soft and delicious — not as open as hoped, but a great eating loaf. Tweaks for next time: stronger gluten work upfront (slap-and-folds or rubaud right after mixing), a tighter final shape, and pulling bulk a little earlier — or a shorter spell outside.
