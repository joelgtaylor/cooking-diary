Milk Kefir Sourdough — A Family Favourite

Nov 9–10, 2026 · two loaves · evening mix · beautifully tender crumb · a glowing review

Ratio

  • 1000 g bread flour
  • About 663 g whole milk kefir, replacing the usual water
  • 200 g sourdough starter
  • 20 g salt
  • Approximately 3 tbsp (45 g) additional water, added after the first mix because the dough felt underhydrated

Two loaves; approximately 1.93 kg total dough before baking. Starter hydration not separately recorded. Kefir contains milk solids, so the nominal liquid-to-flour ratio does not tell the whole hydration story.

Method notes

  • Started the dough in the evening of Nov 9 rather than during the usual open morning schedule. Mixed flour, milk kefir and starter together, holding back the salt.
  • The initial dough felt too stiff; added roughly 3 tbsp of water, judging the dough by feel based on previous batches.
  • Salt added during the second stretch-and-fold round. With limited time for a third or fourth round, it did not always incorporate evenly, leaving a somewhat layered texture in the dough.
  • Despite the imperfect salt integration, the dough developed a smooth, taut surface and the two loaves baked with handsome oven spring, broad banded scores and golden-brown crusts.
  • The crumb was finely and evenly aerated, with a few larger pockets and visible rolling patterns near the lower edge.

Tasting notes

Slightly tangy, a little sweet and buttery almost like brioche. Incredibly tender on the inside, with a pleasantly chewy crust. The milk kefir seems to give the loaf a distinctive enriched-bread character without added butter, eggs or sugar.

“If I was offered Cobb's sourdough or yours, I would choose yours.”
— Vance

Several loaves have also been shared with friends, and the reception has consistently been enthusiastic.

Next-batch experiment

Keep the successful formula almost unchanged, but mix the salt in from the beginning. Premix the 45 g extra water with the kefir for more consistent initial hydration. Try one or two folding rounds as time permits, then judge fermentation by the dough rather than by a rigid schedule. This is a proposed simplification, not how this batch was made.

Result: A standout batch and a family favourite. Rich, lightly tangy, exceptionally tender bread with a chewy crust. Salt incorporation was the main technical frustration, but it didn't prevent an excellent result. Worth keeping as a reference recipe while simplifying the method.
