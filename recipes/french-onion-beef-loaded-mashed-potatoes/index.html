Taylor Family French Onion Beef & Loaded Mashed Potatoes

November 6, 2026Instant PotApproximately 5–6 servings

A family favourite born from two different discounted beef cuts and just one small onion. The stewing beef was especially tender and deeply savoury; the gravy was rich rather than overtly creamy. Served over mashed potatoes seasoned by our daughter entirely to taste.

Ingredients

Beef and gravy

  • 873 g boneless stewing beef
  • 334 g inside round sandwich steak
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced (instead of the originally planned 3 large onions)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced (amount from the cooking plan)
  • 2 tbsp butter and 1 tbsp olive oil, for browning (amounts from the cooking plan)
  • 1½ cups beef broth
  • 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce (revised cooking-plan amount)
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 cup whole milk, added to increase the amount of gravy after pressure cooking
  • 1½ tbsp cornstarch mixed with 2 tbsp cold water, for thickening (original cooking-plan amount)
  • No bay leaf — deliberately omitted

Note: The core ingredients and amounts above follow the recipe discussed before cooking, with the confirmed changes recorded. Exact use of every originally suggested seasoning or thickener was not independently confirmed.

Daughter’s loaded mashed potatoes

  • Potatoes (the original plan was about 1.2–1.5 kg, but the actual amount was not recorded)
  • 2% milk
  • Sour cream
  • Ranch dressing
  • Salt and pepper
  • Garlic powder
  • Italian seasoning

All mashed potato additions were measured by taste, not by spoon or cup. This is a record of what she actually reported using; no quantities are invented.

Method

  1. Thinly slice the one small onion. Sauté in the Instant Pot with butter and oil over medium heat for roughly 15–20 minutes, stirring until deeply golden. Add garlic for the final minute; set the onions aside if needed while browning beef.
  2. Pat the stewing beef dry, season with salt and pepper, and brown in batches on Sauté (High). Return the onion and garlic to the pot.
  3. Add beef broth, Worcestershire, Dijon and thyme; scrape the pot bottom thoroughly to release browned bits. Do not add a bay leaf.
  4. Pressure Cook on High for 40 minutes, then let pressure release naturally for 15 minutes before carefully venting the remainder. This produced the perfect tenderness for the stewing beef.
  5. Meanwhile, cook potatoes in salted water until tender and drain. Mash, then season with 2% milk, sour cream, ranch dressing, salt, pepper, garlic powder and Italian seasoning, adjusting entirely by taste.
  6. Sear the sandwich steak separately in a hot skillet until just cooked, roughly 30–60 seconds per side for very thin pieces. Cut across the grain into strips or bite-sized pieces.
  7. After pressure cooking, add 1 cup whole milk to the gravy to increase the volume. Warm gently. Adjust thickness with a cornstarch-and-cold-water slurry if needed; taste and correct seasoning. Avoid a vigorous prolonged boil after adding the milk.
  8. Fold the seared sandwich steak into the finished gravy and serve the beef and plenty of gravy over the mashed potatoes.

What we learned from the first dinner

  • The stewing beef was the star: perfectly tender after 40 minutes at High pressure, and it took on a tremendous amount of flavour from the broth.
  • The sandwich steak was fine but the weakest part, simply because its flavour did not quite match the richness of the braised beef and gravy.
  • One small onion was sufficient for a delicious dinner when well browned and backed by savoury broth and seasonings.
  • Skipping the bay leaf did not hurt the result.
  • The gravy needed more volume to feed the family. Adding a cup of whole milk solved that problem without making it noticeably creamy; the finished gravy was very rich, brown and glossy.
  • Our daughter made the potatoes entirely by taste, adding 2% milk, sour cream, ranch, salt, pepper, garlic powder and Italian seasoning. Preserve that improvisational approach.

Ideas for the next batch

Not part of the first version.

  • For more evenly flavoured beef, consider using roughly 1.2–1.5 kg of stewing beef and omitting the sandwich steak.
  • If using sandwich steak again, try leaving it in the completed gravy briefly after searing so it can pick up more sauce flavour without a long pressure cook.
  • Keep extra broth or whole milk available to adjust gravy volume after pressure cooking, before final thickening.
  • Balsamic vinegar (1 tsp), brown sugar (½ tsp) and Gruyère were mentioned as optional possibilities in the earlier cooking discussion, but are not confirmed additions to this dinner.
