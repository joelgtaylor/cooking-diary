Milk Kefir Sourdough — Salt from the Start

Nov 16, 2026 · two loaves · simplified mixing · deep golden crust · even crumb

What changed

Another two-loaf batch of milk kefir sourdough, building on the November 10 batch. This time, the salt went into the dough at the beginning of mixing instead of being added during the second stretch-and-fold.

That one change made the process significantly simpler. No separate salt-incorporation step, and no concern about unevenly distributed salt if the schedule gets busy and later folds are missed.

Formula and mixing notes

  • Bread flour: 1,000 g shown on the scale.
  • Sourdough starter: approximately 205 g shown during mixing.
  • Milk kefir: used again as the main liquid; an exact net amount isn't established from these photographs.
  • Salt: included from the initial mix; the amount isn't separately confirmed for this batch.
  • Yield: two loaves.

For comparison, the previous kefir batch recorded 1,000 g flour, about 663 g milk kefir, 200 g starter, 20 g salt, and approximately 45 g additional water. Those figures are a reference, not an assumption that every amount was identical this time.

Process observations

  • The photographs document the kefir-and-starter mixture, measured flour, and a cohesive dough with a smooth, rounded surface.
  • The loaves baked with a well-defined series of banded scores and dark golden-brown crusts. Both held their shape and rose nicely in the oven.
  • The sliced loaf shows a mostly fine, fairly even crumb with a few larger pockets. Some curving structure is visible through the crumb, but no obvious dramatic unincorporated-salt layers.

What to keep

Keep adding the salt with the other ingredients from the start. The earlier method created unnecessary scheduling friction; this change removes a step while still yielding bread that turned out nicely. It is a practical improvement worth carrying forward into future kefir batches.

Result: Two successful loaves. Attractive scoring, nicely browned crusts, and a pleasing crumb. Mixing in the salt from the beginning is the notable win: a significantly more streamlined process without an apparent sacrifice in the finished bake.
